# NAMI Creative — Copy + SEO Overhaul Plan

*Drafted 2026-05-21. References `product-marketing-context.md` for ICP, voice, and proof.*

This plan is opinionated. Where I disagree with the brief, I say so. Where I'm not confident, I flag it. Pick the moves you want to ship and ignore the rest.

---

## 1. Diagnosis: where the message gets diluted

The site is well-built and well-voiced. The problem isn't that it's bad — it's that **it leads with positioning instead of pain**, then repeats the same positioning language at four different abstraction levels. By the time a first-time visitor scrolls to your case studies, they've heard "brand, content, and systems" five ways and "compound" eight times without ever feeling their own problem named.

**Specifics:**

- **Hero is abstract.** `"Creative systems built for real-world momentum."` is positioning, not a hook. A founder landing cold doesn't know if you're for them.
- **`compound` is exhausted.** It appears 15+ times across site copy (services, work, about, values, multiple section heads). The word has lost meaning by repetition. Three of the five service outcomes end with it.
- **`systems` is overused as an abstract noun.** Sometimes it means automation tooling, sometimes content workflows, sometimes the whole studio's philosophy. The reader has to disambiguate every time.
- **Pain isn't named on the homepage** until the positioning band — and that section is currently fixed (we just rewrote it). But the hero above it doesn't set up the pain the band resolves, so the band feels like a non-sequitur.
- **The case studies don't connect back to the visitor's pain.** They're great pieces of work, but a founder reading MILLIONS' brief doesn't think "this is what NAMI would do for me." The bridge is missing.
- **`/about` is the strongest copy on the site** — the "alignment problem" framing in the second paragraph is exactly the diagnostic the homepage needs. It's just hidden two clicks deep.
- **`/contact` form is heavy.** Seven required-or-encouraged fields for a stranger. "Send the brief" assumes the visitor already knows they want a brief. High intent only.
- **No middle-funnel offer.** No on-page newsletter prompt, no "read this first" content offer, no low-commitment Calendly link visible from the homepage. It's start-a-project-or-leave.

**What's actually strong (don't touch):**

- Positioning band (current iteration) — does the pain → answer arc cleanly.
- `/about` second paragraph — the "alignment problem" diagnosis.
- `/process` "The principle" section — `"We launch fast, then stay close."` is canon.
- `/pricing` "Honest pricing" section — disarms the cost objection beautifully.
- The Brand Decay article — best piece of content on the site by a distance.
- Case study templates — structurally tight.
- Tagline `"Waves of Creative Impact"` and `"We Build Brands That Move With Meaning"` — keep both, just stop leading with them.

## 2. The narrative arc to weave through every page

Every page should hit four beats in this order:

1. **RECOGNITION** — name a pain the ICP feels in their bones (founder bottleneck, stitched brand, inconsistent marketing, fragmented tracking).
2. **DIAGNOSIS** — explain the cause in plain language (built by different people, no integration layer, no one sees the whole picture).
3. **ANSWER** — NAMI's framework (self-sustaining creative ecosystem, brand + content + systems by the same hands).
4. **PROOF** — point to a case study, a metric, or a named framework (Brand Decay, Friction Tax).

Action item: the homepage hero currently only hits beat 3. The fix is to start the page on beat 1.

## 3. Page-by-page copy moves

### Homepage hero — the load-bearing change

**Current:**
> H1: Creative systems built for real-world momentum.
> Sub: Brand, content, and systems built to move together and hold while you scale.

**Problem:** Both lines are positioning, not pain. A visitor lands and learns *what NAMI is*, not *whether NAMI is for them*.

**Three options. My pick is Option A.**

**Option A — Founder bottleneck direct (recommended):**
> H1: You're the one holding it all together. **We build the system that takes it off you.**
> Sub: Self-sustaining creative ecosystems for founders. Brand, content, and systems built by the same hands, so your business stops depending on your Friday afternoons.

Why: Names the founder cost in five words, resolves with the NAMI promise in nine. The "Friday afternoons" callback ties to the Friction Tax article — a load-bearing piece of your content cluster. Gradient on "We build the system that takes it off you."

**Option B — Diagnostic, softer:**
> H1: Stop stitching together your brand, your marketing, and your ops.
> Sub: We build self-sustaining creative ecosystems. One studio, one brain, one system that runs without you in every room.

Why: Direct command, less personal. Works if Option A feels too confrontational.

**Option C — Brand-line forward, problem second:**
> H1: We Build Brands That **Move With Meaning.**
> Sub: Self-sustaining creative ecosystems for founders running stitched-together brands, inconsistent marketing, and fragmented tracking. Brand, content, and systems built by the same hands.

Why: Keeps the brand tagline as the hero, lets the subhead carry the pain. The trade is that the H1 doesn't hook on its own — high-intent visitors fine, cold visitors will bounce.

**Recommendation:** Ship Option A. Keep "Waves of Creative Impact" as the eyebrow (already there) and "We Build Brands That Move With Meaning" as the footer / about / sign-off line.

Also: change the secondary CTA from `Explore the work` → `Read: Brand Decay` (or similar low-commitment content link). The current pair is "start project" + "browse" — neither is a soft yes. A content link gives cold visitors a way to engage without committing.

**Reduce friction on first contact.** Currently you have: first name, last name, email, company, project type select, budget select, message. Plus a `Send the brief` CTA that assumes intent.

## 4. SEO + AI-Overviews strategy

### The biggest move: own two terms

You have two genuinely claimable frameworks already named in your content: **Brand Decay** and **Friction Tax**. Most studios don't name their frameworks. These can become Google-search owned terms and AI citation magnets if you build them out.

**Brand Decay** — already a strong article. Make it the cornerstone of a cluster:
- The pillar piece exists. Tune it for AI Overview structure: clear Q&A patterns, FAQ schema, structured data.
- Add four sub-pieces, one per "shape": *Tonal decay*, *Visual decay*, *Promise decay*, *System decay*.
- Each links back to the pillar piece and to the relevant service.
- Within 6 months you can credibly rank for "brand decay" — virtually no competition for that exact phrase.

**Friction Tax** — same play.
- Pillar piece exists (`the-friction-tax.mdx`).
- Add 3-4 sub-pieces: *Admin friction*, *Content friction*, *Sales friction*, *Ops friction* (or similar).
- Each links back, plus into automation-growth service.
- "Friction tax" is more competitive (some founders use it loosely), but you can claim the structured-thinking version of the term.

### Keyword targets — what to chase, what to ignore

**Don't chase (impossibly competitive head terms):**
- `marketing agency`, `brand agency`, `creative studio`, `branding`, `web design`
- `marketing agency newcastle`, `branding newcastle`, `web design newcastle` — even local versions are crowded with budget-tier SMB agencies. Attracting that traffic attracts the wrong ICP.

**Target hard (long-tail, low competition, ICP-specific):**
- `brand decay` — your term to own
- `friction tax` — your term to own
- `creative ops agency`
- `brand and automation studio`
- `creative partner for founders`
- `fractional creative director`
- `self-sustaining marketing system`
- `agency that does brand and content together`
- `brand and systems studio`

**LLM citation targets (problem-aware long-tail):**
- `why is my brand inconsistent across channels`
- `how do I stop my marketing from feeling fragmented`
- `should I hire a brand agency or freelancers`
- `what is brand drift`
- `how do founders stop being the bottleneck`
- `agency vs in-house creative team for early-stage founders`

Each of these becomes either an FAQ, an article, or a section in an article. Tune Brand Decay + Friction Tax to answer the second one ("how do I stop my marketing from feeling fragmented") near the top of each piece — that's the LLM citation gold.

### Schema markup — currently missing, big lift

The site has zero JSON-LD. Add the following site-wide:

**Root layout (`app/layout.tsx`):**
- `Organization` schema. Name, logo, URL, founder reference, social profiles (LinkedIn), contact point.

**`/about` page:**
- `Person` schema for Joe (linked from Organization as founder).
- `LocalBusiness` schema — see local section below.

**Each `/services/[slug]`:**
- `Service` schema with provider linked to Organization.

**Each `/work/[slug]`:**
- `CreativeWork` schema with creator + about (sector).

**Each FAQ section (`/`, `/services`, `/services/[slug]`):**
- `FAQPage` schema with the actual Q&A pairs.

**Each insights post:**
- `Article` schema with author, datePublished, image, articleSection.

**Implementation:** create `components/seo/json-ld.tsx` as a single utility that takes structured props and renders the `<script type="application/ld+json">`. Use it on every page. The `schema-markup` skill in `.claude/skills/schema-markup/` has examples and the validation tools.

### Metadata gaps

Several pages have no explicit metadata and rely on layout defaults:
- `/pricing`, `/about`, `/contact` — add explicit `title` + `description` to each route's `generateMetadata`.
- `/work/[slug]` description currently uses `oneLiner` — that's fine, but check it includes the client name + sector.
- `/services/[slug]` description currently uses `tagline` — fine, but tagline averages ~10 words; meta descriptions should be 140-160 chars for full SERP display.

### Sitemap + robots — already solid

`sitemap.ts` is dynamic, includes everything, and uses real `lastModified` from MDX dates. Robots is fine. No action needed.

### Insights frequency

You have two articles. Both are good. To compete on `brand decay` and `friction tax`, you need 4-8 pieces per cluster. **Minimum monthly cadence, ideally biweekly.** I'd push for two months of one-article-per-week to seed both clusters, then settle into monthly maintenance.

## 5. The Newcastle / North East question — pushback

You floated this as "just a thought." My honest answer: **don't pursue local SEO as a primary strategy. Do add light local trust signals.**

**Why not pursue local head terms:**
- `marketing agency newcastle` and similar attract local SMB buyers shopping on price. Your ICP is founders who care about substance and ship globally. Attracting the wrong traffic is worse than no traffic.
- Local SEO competes against agencies that have been doing local citations for a decade. Even winning takes 12-18 months. The same effort applied to owning `brand decay` pays back in 3-6.
- "Founder in North East England, hiring globally" is part of your story — it earns trust as a footnote. Leading with "Newcastle creative agency" reduces you to a category you've explicitly built away from.

**What I do recommend:**
- Add `LocalBusiness` schema with city (Newcastle if that's where you operate, or just "North East England") for local discovery without local positioning.
- Footer line: `"Built from the North East of England. Working globally."` — one sentence, end of the footer.
- About page: a single sentence acknowledging where you're based. Not a section, just a line in "The Story."
- That's it. No `/newcastle` page, no programmatic local pages, no GMB push as a top priority.

**Exception:** if a meaningful share of your client base IS local and you want to deepen that, we can revisit. From the case studies I see (MILLIONS = Solana, The League = UK-wide, Barking Puppy = global crypto, VESSL = US-ish, Energy Consultants Association = UK-wide), your existing book is largely *not* North East. Stay global.

## 7. What I'm NOT recommending — and why

- **Don't rewrite `/about`.** The page is your strongest copy. Surface it, don't replace it.
- **Don't add testimonial-card style "social proof" to the homepage hero.** Your case studies do this work without ads. Adding "trusted by [logos]" or floating quotes near the hero will cheapen the page.
- **Don't build a `/newcastle` or `/north-east` landing page.** See section 5.
- **Don't add a pop-up newsletter modal.** Pop-ups attract the wrong ICP and read as desperate. The on-page newsletter card on `/insights` is enough.
- **Don't shorten the case studies.** They earn their length through specificity. Founders who care will read them. Founders who won't are not your ICP.
- **Don't add a live chat widget.** Founders who want to talk will email or book Calendly. A chat widget invites tire-kickers.
- **Don't chase short-form social content as a growth lever.** Your content cluster strategy is essay-driven. Doubling down on the format that already works beats spraying across platforms.
