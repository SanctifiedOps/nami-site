# NAMI Creative product context

## What NAMI is

NAMI Creative combines two connected parts of one business:

1. Joe Wilson's creative consultancy, covering brand, websites, content and automation.
2. The NAMI Creative Network, a free directory and community for North East creatives, makers, freelancers and independent businesses.

The common purpose is to help people find their people and get their work seen.

## Who the site serves

- North East businesses and brands looking for practical creative and marketing support.
- Creatives, artists, photographers, musicians, makers, freelancers and independent businesses who want to be found, hired and connected.
- Existing Network members managing profiles, portfolios, events and editorial contributions.
- NAMI's owner, who reviews applications, profiles, events, contributions and operational activity.
- Visitors looking for local creative people, stories and events.

## What people should be able to do

- Understand what NAMI does and why its consultancy and Network belong together.
- Judge Joe's experience and work with confidence.
- Enquire about consultancy work without friction.
- Browse and search the creative directory.
- Join the Network and understand what happens after applying.
- Claim and maintain a member profile.
- Add portfolio work and submit events.
- Draft, preview and submit member-written articles.
- Review and approve Network activity through the admin dashboard.

## Brand character

NAMI is North East-rooted, direct, supportive, energetic and opinionated. It should feel culturally aware without behaving like a trend-led agency template. The voice is plain-spoken, warm and specific. It celebrates real work and real people.

## What NAMI should not feel like

- A generic SaaS product.
- An AI-generated agency theme.
- A purple-gradient technology startup.
- A collection of interchangeable glass cards.
- An exclusive arts institution speaking over its members.
- A corporate networking platform.

## Product principles

- Put real people, work, places and outcomes before decoration.
- Make the next action obvious.
- Keep member-created writing in the member's own words.
- Use animation to explain change or support hierarchy, not to make every surface feel active.
- Keep public pages expressive and product screens calm.
- Preserve URLs, search visibility, structured data, analytics and working user flows during redesign.

## Accessibility and quality baseline

- Target WCAG 2.2 AA.
- Support keyboard navigation, visible focus, reduced motion and native mobile scrolling.
- Use a minimum 16px body size for reading and a practical 12px minimum for secondary interface text.
- Keep tap targets at least 44 by 44 CSS pixels where practical.
- Provide clear loading, empty, success and error states for every asynchronous action.
- Avoid nested landmarks, unlabeled controls and colour-only status communication.
